## Mini self-test assignment

Make your M1 simulator learn the pendulum's **length L and damping b** from measured motion. Like M0, you have inputs, targets, adjustable parameters, and a prediction error. Decide how to connect them yourself.

Write your solution in `calibrate.py`. Choose your own variable names, code structure, helper functions, and debugging experiments. You may reorganize your existing attempt.

## What you have

- `collect_real_data()` supplies starting states `th0`, `om0` with shape `(n,)`, torque inputs `U` with shape `(n, T)`, and measured angles `Y` with shape `(n, T)`.
- Your `rollout(th0, om0, U, L, b)` predicts angles with shape `(n, T)`.
- Your M0 practice demonstrates fitting parameters by minimizing prediction error.

Use the public data-collection function; do not inspect `real_robot.py` for hidden parameter values. Keep the collected observations fixed while fitting the parameters.

## Requirements

1. Keep the interface `calibrate(iters=400, lr=0.05)` and honor its arguments.
2. Start from **L = 1.0** and **b = 0.1**. Optimize their logarithms so the physical parameters remain positive.
3. Use **Adam** to minimize the **mean squared error between simulated and measured angles**. No neural network is needed.
4. Return `(L_cal, b_cal, loss_history)`: two fitted physical values as Python numbers and a list of numeric losses. Choose and document your history convention; its last value must describe the returned final parameters.
5. Print the initial loss, final loss, and fitted values. Save a labeled loss-history plot to `figures/m2_calibration_loss.png`.
6. In WORKLOG.md, record your actual results and briefly explain why you optimize logarithms and how the loss connects to the adjustable parameters.

## Success checklist

- The script runs with `python calibrate.py` in the project environment.
- Both fitted physical parameters are finite and positive.
- The final MSE is finite and lower than the initial MSE on the same observations. Individual updates need not always decrease it.
- Both trainable log parameters receive finite gradients when backpropagation runs.
- The loss history and plot agree with your reported results, including the loss at the returned final parameters.
- Existing simulator checks still pass with `python -m pytest -q`.
- You can explain which parts of M0's learning setup you reused and which you replaced.

A low fitting loss does not prove exact parameter recovery or perfect predictions on every new input. Report what your run demonstrates.
